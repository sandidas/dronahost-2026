# How to store sourdough bread

Store a sourdough loaf cut side down on a board or in a paper bag, at room temperature, away from heat. This keeps the crust crisp and the inside soft for a few days. Do not use the fridge. If you will not eat the loaf soon, slice it and freeze it, then toast slices as you need them.

## Keep a whole loaf at room temperature

Leave the loaf in a paper bag or a bread bin. A plastic bag keeps moisture in and softens the crust. If you want a weekly supply without the planning, see our [bread boxes](/subscription).

## Freeze what you will not eat soon

Slice the loaf first, then freeze the slices in a sealed bag. Take out only what you need. Bakers who want a starter that behaves can read why a [sourdough starter may not rise](/kb/sourdough-starter-not-rising).

## FAQ

### Should I keep sourdough in the fridge?

No. The fridge dries bread out and makes it go stale faster than a counter does. Keep a cut loaf in a paper bag or a bread bin instead. If you cannot finish it in a few days, freezing slices is the better way to keep it good for longer.

### Can I freeze a whole loaf?

Yes, but slicing first is easier. A whole frozen loaf has to thaw for hours before you can cut it. Slices go straight from the freezer into the toaster. Seal them in a bag so the air does not dry them out while they wait.

### Why does my crust go soft?

A sealed plastic bag traps moisture, and that moisture softens the crust. Use a paper bag, or leave the loaf cut side down on a board. If the crust is already soft, a short time in a hot oven will make it crisp again.

### How do I know the bread has gone off?

Look for fuzzy spots or a sour smell that is different from the normal tang. Stale bread is dry but safe, and you can toast it. Bread with any mould should be thrown away whole, because the threads spread inside a loaf where you cannot see them.
